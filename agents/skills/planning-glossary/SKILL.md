Each term below is defined in its source's own words, with the page that prints them, numbered as read_page numbers it. Use the glossary to read a source, and to answer what a term means.

1. Look the term up here first. Give the source's words with their citation, then the meaning in one plain line: optimism bias is "the tendency of individuals to expect better than average outcomes" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 108), so costs are estimated too low.
2. If a term isn't here, look for it with search_sources and read the page. Define it only from a page that states its meaning, quoted with the page. If no source defines it, say "The sources don't define <term>", and stop there: never define a term from memory, however familiar it is.
3. Never guess what an acronym stands for. Expand one only as a source prints it, with the page: an expansion that fits the letters is not evidence. The Ontario Line's cases print "Maintenance and Storage Facility (MSF)" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 45) and never OMSF, so OMSF has no expansion here. The manual's table of acronyms expands many more (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 pp. 8–9).
4. A source can use a term without defining it, as with Class 5 and headway below. Say what the source says, and that it doesn't define the term.
5. A definition isn't a value. Take a rule's value from list_rules, and a project's figures from its own pages.
6. Record a definition only when your instructions ask, quoting it exactly from the page you read.

## Business cases

- Business case: "A generic term for a collection of evidence which, when assembled in a logical and coherent way, explains the contribution of a proposed investment to organizational objectives" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 218). A Metrolinx one has four cases, "Strategic, Financial, Economic, Deliverability/Operations" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 15). The evidence for an investment, in one document.
- Strategic case: "The Strategic Case, which determines the value of addressing a problem or opportunity based on regional development goals, plans and policies" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 8). Does it serve the region's goals?
- Economic case: "The Economic Case, which uses standard economic analysis to detail benefits and costs of the options to individuals and society as a whole, in economic terms" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 8). Is it worth it to society?
- Financial case: "The Financial Case, which assesses the overall financial impact of the options, its funding arrangements and technical accounting issues and financial value for money" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 8). What does it cost and earn, and how is it paid for?
- Deliverability and Operations case: "The Deliverability and Operations Case, which considers procurement strategies, deliverability risks, and operating plans and risks" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 8). Can it be built and run?
- BAU, Business as Usual: "BAU Business as Usual" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 8), "the baseline against which options are compared where the investment has not occurred and existing business practices, committed plans and general trends continue into the future" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 21). The future without the project.
- IBC, Initial Business Case: "the Initial Business Case is the first of four business cases completed in an investment's lifecycle" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 14). "It reviews options to address the problem statement and selects a preferred option for further design and analysis" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 14). Compare the options, and pick one to design.
- PDBC, Preliminary Design Business Case: its first aim is "advancing the recommended option from the Initial Business Case (IBC) to a higher level of design and development (typically 10-30%+)" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 15). The chosen option, designed further.
- FBC, Full Business Case: "the Full Business Case (the next business case in Metrolinx's lifecycle process)" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 115), developed "to advance the project towards final approvals and confirm costs, benefits, and key technical and design choices" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 13). The last case before final approval. The fourth comes after opening: "The PIBC reviews the actual costs and performance of the investment" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 30), the Post In-Service Business Case.
- Level of design: Table 5.5 sets the cases, "IBC PDBC PDBC FBC" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 103), against "Level of Design Completion 0-10% 11-20% 21-30% 31-100%" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 103). How complete the design is: an IBC up to 10%, a PDBC 11–30%, an FBC 31–100%. Table 1.1 gives other levels, about 10% for a PDBC and 10–30% for an FBC (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 27), so say which table you use. Optimism bias goes by Table 5.5.
- BCR, benefit-cost ratio: "The ratio of societal benefit to societal cost, this ratio describes the benefit to society in economic terms, expressed as a return per dollar invested" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 219). Benefit per dollar of cost: above 1, benefits exceed costs. In the PDBC, "Expected Value (EV) is the expected BCR" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 118), "the mean of the continuous distribution of benefit cost ratios obtained by varying the capital cost input" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 114).
- NPV, net present value: "The Present Value of Benefits minus the Present Value of Costs" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 219), a present value being "The real, discounted value of the stream of benefits" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 94), or of costs. Benefits less costs, in today's money.
- Optimism bias, OB: "Optimism bias is the tendency of individuals to expect better than average outcomes" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 108). "In the context of infrastructure projects, optimism bias can lead to underestimation of costs and project duration" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 108). "Metrolinx only adjusts capital costs for optimism bias" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 101), and "OB magnitudes decrease as percent design completion increases" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 102). An uplift on capital cost, for estimates that run low. Take it from list_rules or Table 5.5 (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 103), never from memory.
- Contingency: "Contingencies are included based on estimator's opinion of the confidence interval and base uncertainty associated with the base cost estimate for various scope elements" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 108). Money in an estimate for what isn't known yet. The Ontario Line IBC used a flat rate: "All cost estimates reflect 30% contingency to cover unknown risk events" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 76).
- QCRA, Quantitative Cost Risk Analysis, a name the manual prints across a column break: "Quantitative Cost Risk" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 102), "Analysis (QCRA)" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 102). "A QCRA enhances the accuracy in cost estimation relative to non-QCRA estimates" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 104). An analysis of cost risk that sets the contingency; without one, the contingency is a flat percentage, and Table 5.6 treats "Contingency Supported by QCRA" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 103) and "Absence of a QCRA" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 103) apart. The Ontario Line IBC mentions no QCRA.
- Class 5 estimate: "Cost estimates were prepared on a top-down, factor-based parametric approach and should be considered Class 5 estimates" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 76), and "Ontario Line costs were developed based on conceptual alignments" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 77). The IBC's label for its early, top-down estimates. No source here defines the classes or their accuracy, so give no range.
- Real and nominal: "Real values, used in the Economic Case, reflect the value of the good or service based on escalation without general inflation" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 23). "Nominal values, used in the Financial Case, reflect the expected cost of a good or service in the year of expenditure based on both inflation and escalation" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 23). Real takes general inflation out; nominal is the money of the year it is spent. Give a figure's dollar year, such as 2019$.
- Discount rate: in the manual's words, the social "discount rate reflects society's time preference for money" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 220). Money later counts for less than money now. The rate is in list_rules.
- Rule of a half, ROH: "ROH Rule of a Half" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 9). "The rule of a half is typically applied to calculate the user benefits for new users" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 119): the first rider to switch gains nearly the whole saving and the last nearly none, and "The rule of a half averages out this effect over all new riders to estimate benefits" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 119). A new rider counts for half an existing rider's gain.
- Value of time, VoT: "Value of Time is a factor used to monetize changes in generalized time to determine the overall welfare benefit to transport network users" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 74). What an hour of travel time is worth. The value is in list_rules, in a stated dollar year.
- WEIs, wider economic impacts: "Wider Economic Impacts (WEIs) have emerged internationally as an expansion to economic analysis that explores how a transportation investment can impact both the level of productivity (or economic activity in a region) as well as the distribution of population and employment" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 112). Gains beyond travel time: productivity, and where people live and work.

## Ontario Line acronyms

- GGHM: "GGHM Greater Golden Horseshoe Model" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 8). Version 4, written GGHMv4 or GGHm v4, is "a model used by Metrolinx and the Ontario Ministry of Transportation (MTO) to forecast future transportation demand" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 75). The regional model that forecasts trips, and so riders.
- TPAP: "TPAP Transit Project Assessment Process" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 9). The Ontario Line went "seeking environmental approvals through a TPAP" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 17). Ontario's environmental assessment for transit projects, under "Ontario Regulation 231/08" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 204), passed in 2008. That page's two columns break its sentences, so quote it a line at a time.
- P3, public-private partnership: "P3 Public Private Partnership" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 9). Design-build-finance-maintain, DBFM, "is a form of P3 which is a long-term contract between a private party and a government entity, for providing a public asset and service, in which the private party bears significant risk and management responsibility" (ontario-line-ibc-2019 p. 82). A long contract that moves risk to a private partner.
- MSF: "Maintenance and Storage Facility (MSF)" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 45). Where the line's trains are kept and maintained.
- TPH: "trains per hour (TPH)" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 42). How often trains run.
- TOC, transit-oriented communities: "TOCs will place more housing and jobs near or at transit stations along the routes of the province's four priority subway projects" (ontario-line-pdbc-2020 p. 30). Housing and jobs built at stations.

## TTC service planning

- ANP and ASP: "The plan has been rebranded as the Annual Network Plan (ANP) this year" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 4). "Previously known as the Annual Service Plan (ASP)" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 4). The TTC's yearly plan of service changes, renamed for 2026.
- Major and minor service changes, Board Period: "Minor changes are generally developed and introduced through the Board Period process which occurs approximately every six weeks" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 113), and "TTC makes schedule changes approximately ten times a year during its regular Board Period process" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 6). "Changes which are more substantial, either affecting the travel options of TTC customers or requiring additional resources for operation, undergo a more rigorous review" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 113). "These major changes require TTC Board approval" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 113). The standards list each kind in Table 7 (ttc-standards-2024 p. 22): a minor change fits the equipment and budget there are, and goes in with the regular schedule changes; a major one goes to the Board, through the tests and a trial.
- Span of service and service level: "The span of service (operating hours) and service levels (frequency of service) determine the availability and convenience of transit service for customers" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 13). Span: the hours a service runs. Level: how often it comes.
- Headway: used, but defined in no source. The standards judge frequent service on "scheduled headway intervals" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 17): "a service that operates every 6 minutes is deemed on-time if the headway deviation falls between 3 minutes and 9 minutes" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 17). The manual pairs "Headway or frequency" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 83). The minutes between one vehicle and the next.
- Ten Minute Network: "The TTC's frequent network, known as the Ten Minute Network, is a network of rapid transit, streetcar and bus services that operate every ten minutes or better" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 13). Routes that come that often from about 6 am (8 am on Sundays) to 1:30 am.
- Crowding standard: "Average vehicle crowding sets a standard of comfort for passengers while on board transit vehicles" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 15), set at "the maximum load point, or the greatest number of customers riding at one time, in the busiest direction, along a route during the busiest 60 minutes of each period of service" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 15). "Vehicle crowding standards are applied as an average" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 15). How full a vehicle may be, on average, at its busiest point.
- Boardings per service hour, or service productivity: "Service productivity is a measure of average weighted boardings per revenue service hour" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 114). Each class of route has a "Class average target, based on the average boardings per revenue hour that all routes within each service classification should achieve in each operating period" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 19), and each route a "Route minimum performance target" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 19). Riders boarding per hour of service. The minimums are in list_rules.
- NIA, Neighbourhood Improvement Area: "Neighbourhood Improvement Area (NIA), as designated by the City of Toronto" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 114). "The new policy applies a 125% factor to customer-trips (boardings + alightings / 2) at transit stops in or within 400-metres of residential parts of Neighbourhood Improvement Areas (NIAs)" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 33). An area the City designated; for equity, trips near one count extra.
- Net cost per passenger: "It is defined as the amount of subsidy the TTC requires per boarding passengers, over and above fare revenue collected, to operate a given route" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 20). The plan says the "metric has no defined standards on which basis to pass or fail a proposal" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 115). The subsidy per boarding: report it, don't test it.
- Change in ridership per net dollar, or riders per $100: "The goal of this measure is to ensure that service changes achieve better ridership results than would be achieved through fare changes" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 21). Riders gained per $100 spent, or lost per $100 saved, set against a fare change. The minimum is in list_rules.
- Weighted customer travel time: "Comparison of effect on customers is evaluated based on net change of weighted customer travel time" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 114), over "four stages of the customer journey—walking, waiting, riding, and transferring—each of which are assigned a weight based on customer perception and decision impact" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 114). Time as riders feel it: "1 minute of walking time is equivalent to 1.8 minutes of in-vehicle travelling time" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 39).
- On-time departure, missed trip, short turn: "To be considered on-time, a vehicle must leave its origin timepoint between 1 minute early and 5 minutes late" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 16). "Any vehicle leaving more than 20 minutes late from an end is considered a 'missed trip'" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 17). "A short turn is when a vehicle is turned back and taken out of service before reaching the terminus of a route" (ttc-standards-2024 p. 17). How the standards count a trip as late, lost or cut short.
- Trial period and post-implementation review: "Recommended major service changes are granted a trial period of at least twelve months" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 115). Then "a post-implementation review is conducted to determine whether it has met its targets and should be formalized as a permanent component of the TTC network" (ttc-anp-2026 p. 115). A year on trial, then a check against its targets.
