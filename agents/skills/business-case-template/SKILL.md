The source is Metrolinx's Business Case Manual, metrolinx-bcm-vol2. Page numbers are read_page's, nine above the printed ones. This skill lays out a business case: what each part must hold at its stage. The content comes from the sources, cited, or is marked as a gap.

## The stage

"Four business case stages are required" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 25): Initial, Preliminary Design, Full and Post In-Service. Take the stage from the study or the lead's request. If neither names it, ask; don't pick one. Table 1.1 puts the approximate level of design at "0-10% 10% 10-30%" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 27) for the first three stages, and at 100% (investment has been delivered) for the last. "At all stages of the business case lifecycle, all four cases are considered to a degree commensurate with the stage of evaluation and level of information available" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 28).

## The chapters

A business case should include eight chapters (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 15), and its introduction gives "an overview of the business case and its structure (Context, Investment Options, Strategic Case, Economic Case, Financial Case, Deliverability and Operations Case, and Summary)" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 216). So: Introduction; the Case for Change, the context; Investment Options; the four cases; and the Business Case Summary. Each business case should prepare a concise executive summary that is placed prior to the Introduction Chapter (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 217).

## What each part needs at each stage

Table 1.2 sets it (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 29). The page prints its cells side by side in columns, so a cell's words don't run on: to cite one, quote words from a single printed line of it.

Initial Business Case:
- Context: Define a core problem and/or opportunity to be addressed by transportation investment, the types of benefits this investment can realize, and a range of potential solutions.
- Investment Options: Define a set of investment options based on variations of the preferred option in the strategic business case.
- Strategic Case: Detailed review of the strategic benefits of addressing a the problem with a proposed investment.
- Economic Case: Appraisal of each option including, at a minimum: costs, user, external, and some wider benefits (as relevant).
- Financial Case: Appraisal of each option including, at a minimum: costs, direct revenue impacts, and indirect financial/revenue impacts.
- Deliverability and Operations: High-level appraisal of risks and implementation requirements.

Preliminary Design Business Case:
- Context: Update as required.
- Investment Options: Define specific refinements of the investment selected in the Initial Business Case.
- Strategic Case: Update based on how changes/refinements to the option may alter performance. Note where performance increases or decreases and why. Update based on changes to base case, risks to achieving strategic performance, or other project shaping factors.
- Economic Case: Update based on changes to option specification (runtimes, frequencies, reliability, costs). Note where performance increases or decreases and why. Update based on changes to base case, risks to project achieving economic performance, or other project shaping factors.
- Financial Case: Update based on changes to option specification and emergent risks. Review range of financing approaches and their impact.
- Deliverability and Operations: Detailed review of specific delivery requirements and risks. Review of different procurement programs and their benefits/risks.

Full Business Case:
- Context: Update as required.
- Investment Options: Define a single option that is proposed for delivery.
- Strategic Case and Economic Case: as at the Preliminary Design stage; the table gives both stages one cell.
- Financial Case: Update based on changes to option specification and risks, and define financing approach.
- Deliverability and Operations: Update risks based on option specification. Detail final delivery approach, risk management plan, and benefits realization plan.

Post In-Service Business Case:
- Context: Update as required and compare existing conditions to those forecasted in previous stages of the business case.
- Investment Options: Outline any differences between 'as built' and what was proposed in the Full Business Case.
- Strategic Case: Review annual and to date performance against strategic objectives to determine how accurate the business case was and what could be done to improve performance. Data collection should be used where possible.
- Economic Case: Review annual and to date performance against proxy indicators for economic benefits/costs to determine how accurate the business case analysis was and what could be done to improve performance. Data collection should be used where possible.
- Financial Case: Review actual costs to deliver annual and to-date performance for all key financial metrics. Review benefits of selected financing program.
- Deliverability and Operations: Review risk management, governance structure, and benefits realization plan.
- When: All projects require a Post In-Service Business Case (PIBC) to be completed within two years from the time the project is operational (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 30).

## Each case's sections

- Strategic Case, Table 4.1 (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 79): Introduction; Strategic Evaluation by Outcome Area, one subsection per outcome; Strategic Evaluation Summary, with Option Comparison (Initial Business Case and Preliminary Design Business Case only), Risks and Uncertainty, and Recommendations.
- Economic Case, Table 5.9 (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 110): Introduction; Costs, with Capital Costs and Operating Costs; User Impacts by Mode; External Impacts, with Wellbeing Impacts and Environmental Impacts; Wider Economic Impacts, with Productivity, Imperfect Competition and Labour Markets; Land Value and Development; Economic Analysis Summary, with Economic Appraisal Summary, Option Comparison, Risks and Uncertainty, and Recommendations.
- Financial Case, Table 6.1 (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 184): Introduction; Capital Costs; Operating and Maintenance Costs; Revenue Impacts, with Fare Revenue and Non-Fare Revenue; Labour Force Requirements; Funding Sources; Financial Analysis Summary, with Financial Impact Summary, Option Comparison, Funding Sources and Risks, and Recommendations.
- Deliverability and Operations Case, Table 7.2 (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 200): Introduction; Project Delivery; Operations and Maintenance Plan; Procurement Plan; Deliverability and Operations Case Conclusion, with Risk Review and Recommendations. Table 7.1 gives its core questions at each stage (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 197).

## Drafting

1. Name the stage, then give the outline: each chapter and case, with what the table above says it needs at that stage.
2. Under each heading, give what the sources hold, each with its citation, read from the page first. Where they hold nothing, write the gap and the data needed, such as a regional demand model run for riders or an engineering estimate for cost. Never fill a gap with an invented or estimated figure.
3. Figures are other skills' work: optimism bias and the appraisal rules are metrolinx-business-case's, and a benefit-cost ratio is metrolinx-bca's. Leave them as named gaps for the lead to ask about.
4. Summary chapters do not need to identify a single preferred option (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 217). Recommend nothing: which option goes ahead is a planner's decision.
5. Record nothing unless your instructions ask. Give the outline in your summary.
