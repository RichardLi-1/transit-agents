---
name: metrolinx-business-case
description: Draft or check a Metrolinx business case for a rail project, such as the Ontario Line's Don Mills extension: the four cases, optimism bias on capital cost, the appraisal rules, and which figures are precedents or gaps rather than estimates.
---
A Metrolinx business case is "a collection of evidence assembled into four chapters or cases – Strategic, Financial, Economic, Deliverability/Operations – that explains the rationale for pursuing an investment" (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 15). Cover each case, and say which ones the sources can't fill yet.

1. Take every appraisal rule from list_rules: optimism bias, the discount rate, the value of time and the evaluation period are there, each with its page (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 pp. 103 and 179). Never use a value from memory.
2. Optimism bias goes on capital cost, and in the economic case only (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 102). At the Initial Business Case stage, 0 to 10% design, it is 64% (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 103). The financial case shows the cost without it.
3. When a cost estimate already holds a contingency and has no cost-risk analysis (QCRA), Table 5.6 adjusts the uplift for it (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 103). Its printed formula reads inverted, so record the adjusted cost as a derived figure and hand the formula to a planner to confirm.
4. Values are real dollars of the year of evaluation (metrolinx-bcm-vol2 p. 179). A cost in 2019 dollars and a value of time in 2021 dollars need rebasing before they are set against each other: say so, and don't rebase without an index the sources give.
5. A figure taken from another line, such as its cost per km or riders per km, is a precedent, not an estimate: label it so. Riders need a regional model run and cost an engineering estimate. Until then, mark each as a gap and name the data needed. Never invent one.
6. A benefit-cost ratio needs this project's own benefits and costs, discounted at the published rate over the evaluation period. Another project's ratio isn't this one's: never give it as this project's.
7. Record figures only when your instructions ask: cited figures with quotes exactly on their pages, and derived figures with formulas over them and the rules.
